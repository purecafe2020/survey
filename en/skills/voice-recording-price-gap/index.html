Same Mandarin Recording: 24 RMB or $17 an Hour?

It is not that domestic channels are crooked; the same audio is simply priced differently at each position in the chain. Knowing the position matters more than finding a platform.

Verified：November 2026

Put the numbers side by side first

International platform listing (RWS-type voice project)About $17-18/hrHourly, task-based, PayPal or bank
International crowd project (CrowdGen-type)About $6.4-16 per clip per personPer accepted clip
Domestic intermediary24-48 RMB/hr, two-person pair, 24 eachHourly, weekly, app withdrawal

The same Mandarin audio spans more than five times. Note the third row: two-person pairs. You spend two hours, the pair bills two hours, and each of you takes one hour's pay. That structure is common in dialogue collection.

Four layers, each taking a cut

Model companies do not hire recorders directly. They contract a data vendor, which subcontracts a localisation firm, which finds people through intermediaries or groups. You sit at the far end.

Each layer does something different and takes a different share:

  • Data vendor: sets the spec and acceptance criteria, carries delivery liability;
  • Localisation firm: organises execution, runs QC, fronts the settlement;
  • Intermediary or group: recruits, assigns, chases;
  • You: record and redo.

So 24 RMB an hour is not malice; it is being furthest from the buyer. Move up one layer and the rate changes immediately, but so does the responsibility for finding people and passing acceptance, which is where the spread actually comes from.

Three counting methods, a two-fold difference

Before joining, ask exactly one question: does "per hour" mean sitting hours or effective hours?

MethodYour real rate depends onCommon loss
Sitting hoursWhether the queue is fullWaiting and setup are unpaid
Effective hoursFirst-take pass rateNoise, slips and bad rooms are redone and unpaid
Per accepted clipPass rate x rateRework unpaid; opaque standards hurt most

A concrete case: "record 4-10 clips, 1-2 minutes each", paid per accepted clip. You record ten, six pass, you spent 1.5 hours. That is how a "$16 an hour" listing lands at $6-8 effective.

What actually sets the price of one clip

Four variables, in order of impact:

  1. Language and dialect scarcity: Mandarin is cheapest; Cantonese, Hokkien, minority languages and accented Mandarin pay clearly more.
  2. Device and room: some projects restrict the handset model because training needs a specific microphone response. Mismatch filters you out before skill matters.
  3. Scene complexity: solo read-aloud is the floor; natural two-person dialogue, call-centre audio with background noise, multi-speaker switching all step up.
  4. Compliance cost: projects requiring consent forms, ID and usage limits pay more because fewer people will do them.

So the way to raise your rate is not volume, it is becoming the scarce variant: a dialect, a device, a difficult scene.

Going international: four gates first

  1. Identity: ID plus selfie, VPN off throughout. Mismatched details are rejected outright.
  2. Payout: PayPal or bank transfer, name matching the registration. Mismatch is the top cause of frozen balances.
  3. Device compliance: handset model, OS version, microphone requirements. These are hard filters, not preferences.
  4. English instructions: the spec is in English; misreading one line can return a whole batch. You need enough to parse "no reading voice" precisely.

Pass all four, then talk about rate. Get the order wrong and you waste two hours at gate three.

Domestic channels are usable, if you do the arithmetic

Domestic channels are fast to start, frictionless to communicate with, and settle to WeChat or Alipay. They also pay low with a clear ceiling.

Do three sums before starting:

  • Time: in two-person pairs your input is dialogue length times two, while only one hour bills.
  • Volume: many projects cap you at 10-50 hours total, then you wait for the next batch. Some months you work a few days.
  • Opportunity cost: three hours spent here versus three hours practising an AI-training assessment are not the same long-term return.

The conclusion is simple: use it as the first stop to learn the process and prove the payout chain, not as income.

One line to close

The spread is not a scam; it is an information gap plus the cost of the layers. Two moves are available to you: know which layer you sit on, and find a way up one.

And moving up usually costs no money at all. It costs being able to read an English spec, pass identity verification, and own the handset a project happens to require. Those three beat a hundred extra clips.

Frequently asked

Why do international voice projects restrict the phone model?

Because the model learns the frequency response of a specific microphone. The same voice recorded on a different handset has a different spectrum, and training data must be consistent. The restriction is a technical constraint, not a preference.

What is my recording used for, and is my voice at risk?

Usually to train speech recognition and synthesis. Three things matter: whether the agreement permits synthesising your voice, how long data is retained, and whether withdrawal is possible. Find those clauses before signing.

Is it normal for domestic channels to use WeChat groups?

Common in this work and not inherently wrong. Judge the process, not the channel: legitimate ones never charge first, never ask for bank passwords or SMS codes, and settle with a record you can check. Any upfront fee means walk away.
