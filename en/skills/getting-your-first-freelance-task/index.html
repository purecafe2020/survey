Getting Your First Paid Task: Samples, Platforms and Pricing Nerve

The usual blocker is not skill. It is having nothing to show, which leaves the buyer unable to judge risk and inclined to either lowball or move on.

Verified：November 2026

Build three samples before shopping for platforms

Buyers are judging the artefact, not your software literacy. Prepare three:

  1. A timed subtitle file (SRT) built from a public talk, under ten minutes.
  2. A 500-word before/after proofread showing what errors you catch.
  3. A 60-second audio clip of you reading a passage clearly, as self-verification for voice or transcription work.

Two hours of work in total, replacing most of a CV.

Four entry points, ranked by realistic success

Entry pointCharacterDifficulty
Local research or fieldwork firmsConstant transcription and subtitle needs, reliable paymentLow
Niche professional communitiesLess competition, referral-drivenMedium
Freelance marketplacesVolume, but brutal price comparisonHigh
Open recruitment posts on social mediaMixed quality, needs screeningHigh

Most beginners only look at the third row, when the first has the highest hit rate: those firms always need hands and are bad at squeezing price.

Pricing nerve: do not open with your lowest number

The instinct is to undercut to win the first job, and it nearly always backfires: low prices attract the most demanding clients, and raising them later means finding new clients.

Quote the market middle and be explicit about scope. A clearly bounded scope (word count, runtime, format, revision rounds) wins more work than a 20% discount.

Three traps to avoid

  • Large unpaid tests. Anything over 300 words is free labour; decline.
  • Delivery before any written agreement. Get the deliverable, price and payout date confirmed in writing.
  • Anything that asks you to pay first, whether deposit, software fee or security bond.

Frequently asked

Can I work without experience?

Yes, with samples in place of experience. Three artefacts cost about two hours and beat any self-description.

What should I charge?

Find the market middle, then quote it with a tightly defined scope. Do not open with a discount.

What matters on a first job?

Put deliverable, price and payout date in writing, and refuse free tests over 300 words.
