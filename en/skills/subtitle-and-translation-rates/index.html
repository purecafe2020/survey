How Subtitle and Translation Work Gets Priced

Ten minutes of video can be quoted at $12 or $60. The difference is not the language; it is what you hand over and who accepts it.

Verified：November 2026

Three pricing units, three risk profiles

Per minute for subtitles: the buyer pays for finished runtime, and you carry the risk of unintelligible audio and repeated rewinds. Price that rewinding cost in when the source has heavy accents or jargon.

Per word or per thousand words for documents: transparent, comparable, and the most crowded segment. Chinese to English usually pays more than English to Chinese because the fluency bar is higher.

Per hour for consulting, proofreading or timeline work: rare on one-off jobs, normal in ongoing relationships.

The real price split is the deliverable

DeliverableExtra effortRate effect
Plain transcriptBaseline1.0x
Timed subtitle file (SRT)+30-50%1.3-1.5x
Bilingual subtitles+60-80%1.6-1.8x
Translation from audio (no source text)+50-100%1.5-2.0x
Glossary-enforced terminology+20%1.2x

The classic mistake is quoting for a transcript and delivering bilingual subtitles. Agree the deliverable in writing before you start.

The three rejections that actually happen

Line length. Single-line character counts and characters-per-second are hard limits; exceeding them means re-timing, which often takes longer than the translation itself.

Terminology drift. Rendering one term as A early and B later gets an entire file returned on glossary projects.

Timeline drift. When there is a logo animation at the head, the first subtitle lands early and everything after it is out of sync.

All three are preventable with a five-minute self-check: line counts, glossary, first cue alignment.

Which jobs to decline

  • An unpaid test translation over 300 words with no contract, which is free labour with extra steps.
  • Per-word pricing on multi-speaker audio with heavy accents, where rewinding eats the margin.
  • No stated deliverable, just "use your judgement", which guarantees revisions.
  • Asking you to carry copyright and compliance risk without paying for it.

Frequently asked

How are subtitles usually quoted?

Per minute of finished runtime is most common, then per word. Judge audio clarity and jargon density before quoting per minute.

Why does Chinese to English cost more?

Buyers demand higher fluency in English, and rework is more likely, so the price carries that risk.

Can I get work without a portfolio?

Yes, but build two or three samples first: a timed subtitle file and a document with consistent terminology. Samples beat CVs here.
