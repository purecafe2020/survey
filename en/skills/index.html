Skill work: per-task jobs that pay $5-20 an hour

This track does not ask who you are, only whether what you deliver passes. Lowest barrier, lowest ceiling, and the fastest route to a first dollar.

Verified：November 2026
Compliance note: voice and video collection involves consent for your voice and likeness. Read the data-use, retention and withdrawal terms before signing up, and never retain or reuse client content from transcription work. Anything that asks you to pay a deposit, buy equipment, or buy "access" is not a legitimate channel.

How this differs from the other two tracks

Expert calls sell who you are; AI training sells how you judge; this track sells whether what you deliver passes inspection.

Three concrete differences:

  • Substitutability: hundreds of millions can record Mandarin; far fewer can judge model Chinese. Hence the lower rate.
  • Unit of pay: per clip, per minute, per accepted item, never per hour. Your efficiency sets your effective hourly.
  • Time to first payment: light onboarding, low gate, often a first job within a week, which is its real value.

The right framing: a cash-flow entry point and a training ground, not a long-term income.

Why the same job differs five-fold

The sharpest contrast: recording Mandarin can list at $17-18 an hour on an international platform, while domestic intermediaries commonly pay 24-48 RMB an hour, with two people splitting it.

That is not a scam, it is the cost of the layers in between. Data travels through:

  1. The data vendor holding the model company's contract, spec and acceptance criteria;
  2. The localisation or annotation firm subcontracting, running QC, carrying delivery risk;
  3. The crowd intermediary or group recruiting, assigning, settling;
  4. You.

Each layer takes a cut and absorbs the risk of finding people and failing acceptance. Your position in that chain sets your price.

Which explains something simple: domestic rates are low not because you are cheap, but because you are furthest from the buyer.

Three ways of counting, three different outcomes

MethodHow it countsWhere it bites
Per hourTime you actually workUsually "effective hours": chat, retakes and noisy rooms are unpaid
Per clipOne payment per recording or taskLooks generous, but a clip may be 1-2 minutes and require retakes
Per accepted itemOnly passing submissions are paidRework is unpaid; a low pass rate halves your effective rate

When you see "per hour", ask whether it means sitting hours or effective hours. The gap is half your income.

Three traps that catch beginners

  1. Unpaid "auditions" that want a full deliverable. A real test is 30-60 seconds. Anyone asking for a complete set before "selecting the best" is usually harvesting free content.
  2. Deposits, equipment fees, training fees. Legitimate channels only ever pay you. A 199 RMB "access fee" is the classic scam in this category.
  3. A black-box pass rate. Some channels never publish acceptance criteria and reject indefinitely. Before joining, ask three questions: what the standard is, whether partial payment applies to a failed submission, and how disputes are handled.

How to actually pass (the practical part)

Pass rates here are mostly decided in the ten minutes before delivery:

  • Room: air conditioning off, fan off, window shut, phone in airplane mode, a blanket on the wall to kill echo. Beats a new microphone.
  • Device: some projects restrict the model. One voice project explicitly requires an iPhone 17 series phone because the model trains on that microphone signature. If your handset is not on the list, you are filtered out before skill matters.
  • Spec: read the instructions twice, especially "no reading voice", "natural pace", "one second of silence between clips". Most rework is a spec that was not read.
  • Submission: wrong filenames, a missing clip, wrong format. Whole batches get returned. The most frustrating failure, and the most common.

Should you do this at all

Three lines to decide:

  • Want a first dollar within a week: yes, fastest of the four tracks.
  • Want it to cover living costs: no. Piece rates plus rework often land under $5 an hour.
  • Want long-term income: treat it as a springboard. Rehearse "deliver to spec" and "get paid across borders" here, then move up to AI training or expert calls.

One line to keep: this track pays for execution, not expertise. Accept that and it will not disappoint you.
