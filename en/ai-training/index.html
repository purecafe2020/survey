AI training work: judge the model, fix the Chinese, get paid by the hour

No interviewer, no survey quota. You read a piece of model output, decide whether it sounds like a human wrote it, and explain why. Platforms pay by the hour or by the task. The gate is not a degree; it is written English and patience.

A listed rate is the platform's own band, usually a starting or ceiling figure, not your effective hourly. Real income depends on how many tasks you are given, your quality score and whether your region has projects at all.

What the work actually is

A model produces an answer; a person decides whether it is good. Three task shapes dominate: compare two answers and pick the better one, rate an answer from one to five and explain the score, and rewrite what fails. The industry calls this human feedback; in practice it is writing a reusable definition of quality.

Native Chinese speakers are valuable here for a specific reason. Model Chinese is often grammatically perfect and idiomatically wrong: mismatched measure words, chengyu dropped at the wrong pitch, and a register that turns every reply into a product manual. A native reader spots it instantly, but explaining it takes writing. The skill is not knowledge volume; it is translating instinct into a reason.

How the money works: a ceiling, not a wage

Platforms publish a band per track, not a salary. DataAnnotation lists $25-40 an hour for language work, $25-50 for general, and $40-150+ for coding. The spread reflects difficulty and scarcity, and you must pass the assessment for a track before you see its work.

The harder constraint is volume. Projects open and close, and gaps of several weeks with no matching work are normal. So the real figure is the hourly rate multiplied by the hours you can actually get, and you do not control the second number. Treat it as hourly piecework, not a wage.

The third row is the hard part. A rejected screener at least tells you the quota was women under 30. Quality review on an AI platform is opaque: one day the queue is empty and nobody says why. Which is why the first rule is do not put all your hours in one platform.

Four gates everybody passes

Onboarding is remarkably similar across vendors, and so is where people get stuck:

  • Identity check. A government-issued photo ID that has not expired, plus a live selfie, usually through Persona. VPNs must be off for the whole process.
  • Payout account. PayPal, Payoneer or Tipalti. The account name must match your registration, and a mismatch is the most common reason balances get frozen.
  • Tax form. Non-US contributors normally file a W-8BEN. This is a compliance requirement, not an option.
  • Region. The hardest gate. Work is allocated by country, and if your region is not served, skill does not help. Never spoof it with a VPN or a fake address: identity checks compare documents against network data, and the outcome is a ban plus a frozen balance.

Who should, and who should not

It fits people who can write full English sentences explaining why a paragraph fails, who can live with a silent assessment period and fallow project weeks, and who will follow instructions line by line.

It does not fit anyone who needs money next week, anyone counting on it for rent, anyone unwilling to verify identity or file a tax form, and anyone whose English is read-only—your deliverable is the explanation, so if you cannot write it, you have not done the work.

One line to keep: AI training pays you for explaining why; paid research pays you for who you are. Both need patience, spent in different places.
