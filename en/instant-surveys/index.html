What 口子查 really is: instant paid surveys

口子查 translates as Instant Paid Survey: brands and research firms post their own standalone links, whoever sees it first takes it, and payment lands on completion. It rewards timing and speed, not answering skill.

Verified：November 2026

What it is and where the money comes from

A company needing survey data has two routes: hire a research firm, which produces panels and reseller channels, or post the link from its own account on Facebook, Twitter, LinkedIn or Reddit. The second route skips the middleman, and part of the saved commission goes to respondents, which is why unit prices look higher than panel rates.

The signature traits: standalone link, no registration, a fixed quota, and immediate closure once the quota fills. Hence the Chinese nickname 秒付查, literally "instant-pay survey", because many pay a gift card code or PayPal transfer on the spot.

Three types with very different economics

TypeMechanismWhat actually landsWorth doing
Guaranteed rewardPaid on completion, usually a gift card$5-20Yes, the most reliable
Instant payPaid immediately after qualifying$5-50, recruitment studies can reach $100+Yes, if you catch it early
Prize drawEntry into a draw, paid only if you winNear-zero expected valueNo, time for a lottery ticket

Many guides lump all three together as "you can earn from instant surveys". The real experience differs enormously. Prize draws can have an expected value under one cent, which is exactly why most people quit after a few days.

Finding them through public channels

These links are published publicly, so finding them is not a violation. Search social platforms for live recruitment.

  • Keyword patterns: survey plus category (skincare, automotive, gaming), "research study" plus a city, "paid study" plus a job title, "participants needed".
  • Where: Facebook groups and pages, Reddit's r/beermoney and category subreddits, LinkedIn posts by researchers, X accounts of research firms.
  • How to filter: sort by recency and only take posts under 48 hours old; prefer ones stating the incentive; prefer ones naming a company and a privacy notice.

One fact to accept: quotas often fill within hours. You are racing information, not difficulty. That is also why it cannot be your main income, since earnings depend on whether you happened to see a post.

Getting through the screener

Higher-paying instant studies, especially interview recruitment, still screen you. Four principles:

  1. Read every question before answering. Screeners contain reversed items (asking about cats, then about dogs) and contradictions eliminate you.
  2. Answer what is true. A guessed profile collapses under follow-up questions in the session: which brand, how often, who decides.
  3. Do not rush. Submitting in under half the stated duration reads as careless and gets voided.
  4. Write specific open answers. "It was fine" scores low; "I last bought X, then switched to Y because X got too expensive" reads as a real respondent.

Five scams to walk away from

  • Pay-first schemes: membership, deposit or an unlock fee. Legitimate studies never charge.
  • Prize draws dressed as guaranteed pay: promised on completion, then quietly entered into a draw you never win.
  • Requests for ID scans plus bank cards: real research asks for a name and email at most, never card details or passwords.
  • Selling "insider links" or "survey packs": paid link lists are usually expired or phishing, and buying them breaches platform terms anyway.
  • Links arriving by text or DM: check the domain before clicking anything unsolicited.

Who it suits, honestly

Suits: people with fragmented time who react fast and will spend twenty minutes a day watching feeds, especially those with other income who treat it as opportunistic.

Does not suit: anyone needing predictable income, since quotas are uncontrollable; anyone unwilling to gamble on prize draws, which make up much of the supply; anyone hoping for thousands a month, because the people earning that have deep-research sessions, not faster clicking.

The sane structure: instant surveys as a supplement, deep research as the main line. The first gives cash flow and small wins; the second gives the real unit price.
