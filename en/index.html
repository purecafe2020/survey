The tiers differ by two orders of magnitude for one reason: the harder you are to find, the more you are paid. Deep research does not pay for questions answered; it pays for being a rare sample who will block out an hour.

Why it costs more

The screener is the pricing valve

Every deep-research session is gated by a screener. It is not a test of you; it verifies three things: whether you qualify (say, whether you are an enterprise IT decision-maker), whether quota remains (each segment typically recruits 8-12 people), and whether you are answering carefully (consistency, trap questions, open text).

The critical fact: screeners are unpaid on Respondent, User Interviews and UserTesting alike. Income is therefore sessions qualified times session fee, not hours times rate. That is exactly why most people quit after two weeks: they apply a survey-panel hourly mindset to what is fundamentally a lottery with a good ticket price.

Our position

Only methods that pay out and stay compliant

A lot of Chinese-language content in this space teaches proxy switching, multiple accounts, filling in for others and buying leaked links. We publish none of it, not from moralising but from three practical outcomes:

  • You will not get paid. Risk systems cross-check IP, device, payment account and profile consistency; mismatches mean withheld incentives and forfeited balances.
  • The account dies. One flag ends future invitations, which burns the long-term ticket.
  • It is legally exposed. Misrepresenting identity to obtain research incentives is fraud in several jurisdictions, not grey-area side work.

What we do cover: finding brand-posted recruitment through public channels, describing yourself accurately, recognising scams, and delivering on time so the money lands.
