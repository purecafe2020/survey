B2B interviews: highest pay, hardest gate

The same sixty minutes pays $50 for a consumer study and $250 for a B2B one. The difference is not time; it is whether you are the person who can sign.

Verified：November 2026

Why enterprise interviews pay three to five times more

Three factors stack up: cost of finding you (a company may contain exactly one eligible person), value of the information (their judgement maps to a six- or seven-figure purchase), and opportunity cost (a director who can spare an hour is expensive).

That is why the budget structure differs: a forty-person consumer study might cost $3,000 in total, while a single B2B interview pays $200-400 because the client is deciding whether the product has a market at all.

Ranges are indicative; the study page governs. Within one function, people who own budget earn more than double those who only use the tool.

Work email: where half of applicants fail

B2B verification leans on three signals: a company-domain email, a LinkedIn profile and a team page on the corporate site. They have to agree.

  • Passes: a @company-domain address, a group subdomain, an active LinkedIn record.
  • Usually rejected: Gmail, Outlook, QQ or 163 addresses unless the study explicitly allows freelancers.
  • Gets flagged: someone else's work email, an address still receiving mail after you left, one address tied to multiple identities.

If you genuinely lack a work email, as a freelancer, a jobseeker or a student, do not force B2B. Apply to consumer interviews or usability tests instead. This tier is not open on request.

How to state your authority: four tiers

TierHow to phrase itWhat it qualifies you for
User"I use the system but I am not in selection"Experience and usability studies
Influencer"I raise requirements and shortlist; the department head decides"Needs and evaluation-criteria studies
Decision-maker"I approve purchases under 500k"Purchase-decision studies, the best paid
Approver"Anything above 500k needs my sign-off"Budget process and vendor management

Describing yourself as an influencer is not a downgrade; many studies specifically want the not-quite-decision-maker who is not a novice either. Claiming decision authority as a user collapses at the first question.

What they will ask, and why invention fails

The interviewer, usually a product manager or researcher, goes specific:

  • When was the last purchase, what was it, what was the budget?
  • Which vendors were evaluated, and why were two of them dropped?
  • Which steps does procurement run through, and who holds veto power?
  • What are the three biggest problems with the current tool?

Those four questions cannot be improvised. Real respondents name months, vendors and frustrations. Invented ones produce "roughly", "it's fine", "a lot", and are disqualified within five minutes, with the incentive cancelled and the account flagged.

Where the B2B volume sits

Respondent is the densest source, with a large share of B2B and credentialed projects and the highest fees. User Interviews has volume but skews consumer and light SaaS. Usability platforms such as UserTesting and Userlytics barely do B2B interviews at all.

Much B2B recruitment is never public: client success teams and industry communities invite people directly. So beyond registering, keeping a coherent, current LinkedIn profile is itself a way of being found.

Three lines not to cross

  • Never borrow someone's identity. Using a colleague's or former colleague's email and role is identity fraud and exposes them too.
  • Never disclose confidential company data. Most studies carry an NDA; you may decline questions about unpublished contract values or vendor quotes and say why.
  • Never split one project across several people. It is the violation recruiters watch for most, and the whole group gets flagged.

The people who last in this tier are not the cleverest; they are the ones whose every sentence survives a follow-up question.
