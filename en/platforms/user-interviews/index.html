User Interviews

The densest interview marketplace: slightly lower fees than Respondent, far more invitations.

If you only sign up for one platform in this category, User Interviews is the sensible first pick. Not because it pays best, since Respondent does, but because of density: its researcher pool includes large numbers of SaaS companies, agencies and university teams, and it adds more interviews and focus groups each week than anyone else. That means more absolute selections, not just better odds per project.

Incentives usually run $50-150 per session depending on length and how rare your profile is. A thirty-minute consumer study may pay 50; a sixty-minute B2B interview can exceed 150. Payment is released after attendance is confirmed, and PayPal settlement is reliable.

It has a control that others lack: you can cap how many sessions you take per week and set a minimum incentive, and the platform filters invitations accordingly. For anyone tired of being flooded with low-value invitations, that is genuinely useful.

The downsides are real. Most studies are US-based, so people on China time zones are taking calls late at night; screeners are unpaid and many projects eliminate you at question three or four; and consumer and social topics repeat heavily, which can turn into a long loop of applying without being selected.

Strengths

  • Highest project density, so more selections in absolute terms
  • Minimum-incentive and weekly-cap settings reduce noise
  • PayPal settlement with a clear history
  • Both interviews and focus groups, not a single format

Weaknesses

  • Mainly US studies, which means awkward time zones
  • Unpaid screeners that often eliminate you early
  • Fees slightly below Respondent on average
  • Consumer topics repeat, which gets tiring

Practical tips

  • Complete your profile fully; matching volume depends on it
  • Set a minimum incentive and hold out for studies worth your time
  • Watch your inbox for 48 hours after applying; many studies fill first-come
  • Widen your stated availability to raise your hit rate

Frequently asked

User Interviews or Respondent?

Register for both. Respondent pays more and skews B2B; User Interviews has more volume and is easier to get a first session on. Start here, then expand.

Do I get banned for failing a screener?

Not for simply being a mismatch. But inventing a profile in the screener and being exposed in the session gets the incentive withheld and the account flagged.

Can users outside the US use it?

You can register, but most studies recruit US participants, so practical opportunities are limited and require taking late-night calls.
