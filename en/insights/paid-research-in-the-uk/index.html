Paid Research in the UK: What's Open and What Pays

The UK is the second-best market in this industry after the US, and in one respect better: the largest research platform is British, pays in pounds, and enforces an hourly minimum.

Registration country matters: UK residents see inventory that Irish or French residents do not, because clients recruit per market.

Why the UK punches above its size

Three things. Prolific was founded in Oxford and pays UK participants natively in pounds with an enforced floor, which almost no other platform does. YouGov is a British polling company, so opinion research runs continuously rather than in bursts. And UK respondents are routinely segmented out in European studies, which means less competition than the same study in the US.

The payout trap that catches people

Everything is fine until currency conversion. GBP to a non-GBP account through PayPal costs roughly 3-4%, and bank transfers add intermediary fees. On a £20 withdrawal that is 60-80p; on small amounts it can eat a fifth of the reward.

Two fixes: accumulate to a sensible threshold before withdrawing, and where the platform offers a GBP wallet or a credit to a GBP account, use it rather than converting.

Where the real money sits

Consumer panels in the UK pay roughly £2-5 an hour, which is better than nothing and worse than almost any other use of the time. Interviews and focus groups pay $40-350 per session through Respondent and User Interviews, and Prolific's academic studies pay £6-12 an hour with a guaranteed floor.

So the same structure applies as everywhere: use panels for gaps, use research platforms for income.

Tax

There is a trading allowance for small miscellaneous income, which covers most casual participants. Above it, income is declarable, and gift cards count at face value. Keep a simple monthly log; reconstructing a year of £3 surveys in April is miserable.

This is general information, not tax advice.

Frequently asked

Is the UK a good market for paid research?

Yes, second only to the US in volume, and better in one respect: Prolific pays in GBP with an enforced hourly floor.

Do I need to be a UK resident?

Yes. Registration country must match where you actually live, and clients recruit per market.

Why does PayPal take so much?

Currency conversion costs roughly 3-4%. Accumulate before withdrawing, or use a GBP-denominated destination.
