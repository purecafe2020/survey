Why Most Paid Survey Sites Only Work in the US

The most common complaint about paid surveys is not low pay. It is that the panel has nothing for you at all. That is not a personal rejection; it is how the market is built.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

The client decides, not the platform

A brand commissioning research wants opinions from the market it sells in. If a company sells shampoo in the United States, it needs American respondents, and a panel cannot substitute a French respondent no matter how fluent they are.

Panels therefore open the countries their clients ask for. The US dominates because it is the largest single consumer market with the largest research budgets, followed by the UK, Canada and Australia, which share language and buying patterns.

Why the US gets everything

Three reasons stack. Budget: most research spend sits with US clients. Sample design: studies are often built around US demographics because the product launches there first. Vendors: the largest panel aggregators are US-based and recruit locally by default.

This is why a site can look global on its homepage and still have zero inventory for anyone outside a handful of countries. The marketing is global; the fieldwork is not.

The wide-coverage platforms are the exception and worth knowing: UserCrowd reaches 124 countries, Toluna around 60, Prolific most OECD countries plus a handful of others.

How to check your own country in two minutes

  1. Open the platform's help centre and search for "countries" or "eligible". Panels publish a list; if they do not, assume you are excluded.
  2. Register and look at the study list after a week. Zero studies in seven days means the answer.
  3. Check the payout country list separately. Some platforms accept registrations globally but only pay in supported markets, which is the worst version of a yes.

What never fixes it

Proxies and borrowed identities do not create demand. They trigger a risk check, which cross-references IP history, device locale and payout account, and the result is a withheld payment plus a ban.

If your country is not served, the realistic move is to change category rather than change your apparent location: usability tests and diary studies travel better than nationally-targeted consumer panels.

Frequently asked

Why do I see no surveys at all?

Almost always because your country is not in the client's target market. Panels show global marketing but recruit per market.

Does a VPN help?

No. Risk systems compare IP, device locale and payout country, and mismatches mean withheld payment plus a ban.

Which platforms have the widest coverage?
