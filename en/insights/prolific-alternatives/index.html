Prolific Alternatives: Five Platforms Worth Trying

Most people looking for Prolific alternatives are not unhappy with Prolific. They are stuck on the waitlist, or live somewhere it does not serve. Here is what to do about it.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

Why people leave (or never get in)

Three reasons, in order of frequency: the waitlist (roughly 13% of applicants are invited, and support cannot move you up), country eligibility (most OECD countries plus a few others), and droughts (supply depends on what researchers happen to be running).

None of these has a workaround. Proxies and second accounts end in a ban with the balance forfeited, which is worse than waiting.

That ordering is deliberate: interviews first, because they pay the most per hour of anything available without credentials.

The honest difference

No alternative has Prolific's enforced hourly floor. Prolific requires researchers to pay at least £6/$8 an hour and recommends £9/$12, calculated from real median completion times. Everywhere else, the reward is whatever the client set, and a badly priced study is simply a badly priced study.

What replaces the floor is session pricing. A $100 interview for sixty minutes is $100 an hour whether or not anyone guaranteed it.

What to do while you wait

  1. Register for User Interviews and UserTesting today; both onboard immediately.
  2. Complete every profile field. Matching is field-based, and an empty occupation field disqualifies you from half the inventory.
  3. Set a minimum incentive where the platform allows it, so you stop being invited to $5 studies.
  4. Leave the Prolific waitlist alone. Refreshing it does nothing.

What not to do

Do not buy an "invitation", do not use a proxy to appear local, and do not open a second account after a rejection. Each of those converts a temporary inconvenience into a permanent exclusion, and in the case of proxy use, into a forfeited balance.

Frequently asked

Which alternative is closest to Prolific?

In feel, User Interviews: research studies, honest incentives, an immediate sign-up. In pay structure nothing matches the enforced hourly floor.

Are any of them as fair as Prolific?

None publishes an enforced minimum. The closest thing to fairness is session pricing, where the fee is stated before you accept.

Can I run all five?

You can register for all five. Realistically you will do interviews on two, tests on one, and AI training when projects are live.
