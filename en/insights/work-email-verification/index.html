Why Research Studies Ask for a Work Email

Half of B2B research applicants fail at the first gate, and it is not the questions. It is the email address. Here is what platforms actually check and what to do if you do not have a corporate inbox.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

What the check is really for

Researchers pay hundreds of dollars for a session because they need a real practitioner, not someone who has read about the category. The work email is a cheap proxy for "this person actually works there": the domain proves the employer, and the verification link proves the inbox is live.

It is also fraud control. The single most common way people fake a B2B profile is borrowing an address, so the domain-plus-LinkedIn cross-check exists to stop exactly that.

What passes

  • A company-domain address: you@company.com or you@company.co.uk.
  • A group subdomain, such as you@brand.group.com, where the parent is recognisable.
  • An address that matches an active LinkedIn role and a team page on the corporate site.

The strongest applications have all three agreeing. One signal alone is often enough for consumer-adjacent studies, but enterprise studies check harder.

What fails

SignalWhat happens
Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo, QQ, 163, Mail.ruFiltered out unless the study explicitly allows freelancers
An address from a former employerRejected at review, sometimes flagged as misrepresentation
Domain that does not match the stated employerCross-check fails, application voided
One address used for several identitiesRisk flag, potentially a ban
Disposable or alias domainsBlocked by most verification services

Do they read my email?

No. Verification is transactional: the platform sends a link or code, you click it, and the check is complete. Some systems also look up the domain's mail records to confirm the company exists. Nobody reads your inbox, and no legitimate platform asks for your password.

Anything that does ask for a password, or for a code you were not expecting, is phishing rather than research.

If you are freelance or between jobs

Three honest options, in order of effectiveness:

  1. Use your registered business domain. A consultant with a real company site and a matching address qualifies for some studies, and correctly declaring yourself as an independent consultant is not a lie.
  2. Apply to roles that do not require employment. Consumer studies, usability tests and diary studies have no such gate.
  3. Wait until you are employed again and fill in the profile properly at that point. This is not a loss; B2B opportunities track your job, not your enthusiasm.

What never works is borrowing a friend's address. It exposes them, voids the session, and gets both accounts flagged.

Keep the three signals consistent

Because verification cross-checks, the cheapest reliability upgrade is boring consistency: the job title on the platform, the headline on LinkedIn and the title on the company site should say the same thing. Discrepancies that look trivial to you look like fabrication to a reviewer.

Update all three within a week of changing roles. Stale titles are the second most common cause of B2B rejection after the email itself.

Frequently asked

Can I use a client's domain email?

Only if you genuinely work there in the capacity you describe. Using a client's address to claim employment is misrepresentation and voids the session.

What if my company blocks verification emails?

Ask the research platform about an alternative, such as a LinkedIn cross-check or a call to a public company line. Many can accommodate; none will accept a public mailbox as a substitute for a company one.

Is a university email accepted?

For studies aimed at academics or students, yes. For B2B enterprise studies, usually not, because the client is buying commercial experience.
