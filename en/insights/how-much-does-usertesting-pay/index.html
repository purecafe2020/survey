How Much Does UserTesting Pay? Real Numbers and Rules

UserTesting is the easiest entry point in paid research: no interview, no résumé, no appointment. It also pays the least per hour, and the reasons are entirely mechanical.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

What a test pays

Standard unmoderated tests pay roughly $10 to $60 for fifteen to twenty minutes of work. A plain five-minute task sits at the bottom; a longer multi-task study with a written debrief sits at the top. Live moderated sessions, where you talk to a researcher in real time, pay considerably more and are far rarer.

Payment lands via PayPal about fourteen days after the test is approved, longer if a client is slow to review.

The hardware gate

This is where beginners lose money without understanding why. The platform asks for a desktop with at least 8GB of RAM, a 1920×1080 screen and 5Mbps upload, or a phone with the app installed and at least 20% battery.

Fall short and the recording fails partway through. You still spent the twenty minutes; you are paid nothing. Run the equipment check once, properly, before chasing tests.

Think aloud, do not narrate a performance

Tests are paid for the stream of consciousness, not the verdict. The client wants to hear where you hesitated, which label you misread, which button you expected to exist and did not.

Silent clicking is the most common reason a test is rejected. So is a smooth run-through ending in "that was easy", which tells the client nothing and reads as low effort.

Why tests get rejected

ReasonWhat it looks like
Silent audioMic muted, or you clicked without speaking
Abandoned tasksYou skipped a step the brief required
Too briefFinished in a third of the expected time
No genuine frictionEverything was "fine" and nothing was confusing
Off-briefTested something other than what was asked

Two rejections in a row usually means an audio or hardware problem rather than a judgement problem. Check the recording before submitting the next one.

Realistic monthly earnings

Test availability depends on your country, device mix and profile. In a major English-speaking market, five to fifteen tests a month is a reasonable band, so $100 to $500. Outside those markets it can be near zero for weeks.

Treat it as the cash-flow layer of a portfolio, not the main line. The main line is interviews and focus groups, where the same hour pays several times more.

How to get more invitations

  • Keep the profile current, especially the devices you own; most studies target one device type.
  • Answer the qualification surveys even when they lead nowhere; they feed matching.
  • Submit high-quality tests; approval history influences invitation volume.
  • Leave the app installed on your phone; mobile inventory is larger than most people assume.

Frequently asked

How much does UserTesting pay per test?

About $10-60 for a standard twenty-minute unmoderated test. Moderated live sessions pay more but are much less common.

Why was my test rejected?

Most often silent audio, abandoned tasks, an unusually short runtime, or narration that reports no friction at all.

Can I do tests on a phone?

Yes, and mobile inventory is substantial. Install the official app and keep the battery above 20% before starting.
