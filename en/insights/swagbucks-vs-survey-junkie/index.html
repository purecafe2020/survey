Swagbucks vs Survey Junkie: Which Pays More per Hour?

These two get compared constantly and the answer depends on one question: do you actually shop online? If yes, Swagbucks wins on total value. If not, Survey Junkie is the cleaner tool.

Where Swagbucks actually wins

Not on surveys. Its survey pay is middling and its screen-out rate is high. It wins because it is an ecosystem: if you were going to buy something online anyway, routing the purchase through its link earns faster than answering questionnaires for an hour.

That is a different activity, and it is the honest reason to join. As a pure survey tool it is unremarkable.

Where Survey Junkie wins

Clarity. One activity, one exchange rate, a $5 floor and no offer walls. If your goal is to convert ten minutes into a small amount of money without being nudged toward gambling, app installs or low-value videos, it is the better instrument.

It is also easier to compute an honest hourly rate, which makes it easier to decide whether the time is worth spending.

The real comparison is against something else

Both sit in the same tier, roughly $1-6 an hour, so the difference between them is small. The larger decision is whether to spend that hour on panels at all, or on a research platform where a single session pays $40-150.

This is not a rhetorical point: measured per hour, one qualified interview equals a month of diligent panel work.

Verdict

  • Already shop online, want passive value: Swagbucks, using cashback rather than surveys.
  • Want clean survey work with a low floor: Survey Junkie.
  • Want money rather than points: neither; go to interviews and focus groups.

Running both is fine and costs nothing but an extra inbox. Neither requires exclusivity.

Frequently asked

Which pays more per hour?

Swagbucks surveys average slightly higher, roughly $3-6 against $1-4, but its screen-out rate is worse and its value comes from cashback rather than surveys.

Which pays faster?

Survey Junkie at $5 is quicker to reach. Swagbucks gift cards start around $3 but PayPal requires a higher balance.

Can I use both?

Yes, and there is no penalty for doing so. Just keep one account per platform; duplicates are what get people banned.
