Your Survey Site Won't Pay: Eight Reasons, In Order

A withheld payout is usually a mechanical problem, not theft. Work through these eight causes in order; the answer is almost always in the first three.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

1. You have not hit the threshold

The most common reason by a wide margin. A balance of $4.20 on a $5 floor cannot be withdrawn, and no amount of support contact changes that.

Check the actual threshold for your chosen payout method, not the marketing figure. Gift cards are often available at a lower floor than PayPal.

2. Your identity does not match

Name, date of birth or country on the profile versus the payment account. A married name, a business PayPal, an old address or a VPN that shifts your apparent country will all hold a transfer.

Fix the data rather than arguing: align the legal name, country and currency across profile and payment account.

3. A quality flag on your responses

Completion times far below the median, straight-lining option positions, contradictory answers or identical open text all flag a submission. The platform withholds payment and often does not tell you which flag fired.

This is appealable, but only with specifics: the study name, the date, and a calm statement of what you did.

4. Duplicate accounts

Two accounts linked by device, payout method or address results in both being frozen, and balances are usually forfeited. This is the single most expensive mistake in this industry.

If you suspect a duplicate exists, contact support before withdrawing rather than opening another.

5. Payment method problem

An unverified PayPal account, a bank account in a different name, a closed card, or a currency the processor does not support. Each looks like a platform failure from your side and is invisible to the platform until they try to send.

Test with the smallest available payout first, to the method you intend to use permanently.

6. Country restriction

Some platforms accept registrations globally but only pay in supported markets. The terms usually state this in a table nobody reads until the first withdrawal.

If you are outside the supported list, this is not resolvable; redirect your effort to platforms that serve your market.

7. Expired rewards

Some incentives carry an expiry, and dormant balances can be closed under terms that mention inactivity periods. These clauses are legal and enforced.

Practical rule: withdraw at the threshold rather than accumulating. A balance on a platform is exposure, not savings.

8. It is actually a scam

If none of the above apply, and the site has no named company, asks for a fee to unlock withdrawal, or cannot produce a single verifiable payout record, you are dealing with fraud rather than a dispute.

Stop engaging, change any reused passwords, and report it to your national consumer protection or cybercrime channel.

How to escalate properly

One support ticket with the facts: study name, date, amount, and what you have already checked. Ask for a specific action, not an explanation. If there is no reply in ten business days, escalate once to a second channel, then decide whether the platform is worth your time.

Sunk-cost thinking is the real risk here. A site that will not pay $20 after two complaints will not pay $200 later.

Frequently asked

Can I recover a frozen balance?

Sometimes, if the cause is an identity mismatch you can correct. Balances from duplicate accounts or confirmed quality fraud are usually forfeited permanently.

How do I report a site that will not pay?

Use your national consumer protection agency or cybercrime channel, and leave a factual public review. Keep screenshots of the balance and the support thread.

Should I keep using it if payment is late once?

Once is normal. Withdraw at the threshold from then on rather than accumulating, and stop entirely if the same problem repeats.
