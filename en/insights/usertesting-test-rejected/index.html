Why Was My UserTesting Test Rejected?

A rejected test costs you twenty minutes and, more importantly, feeds a rating that decides how many invitations you get next month. Almost every rejection is fixable.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

How approval actually works

You record, submit, and a reviewer, sometimes the client, sometimes the platform's quality team, decides whether the recording meets the brief. Approval triggers payment; rejection means no payment and a note on your account.

Ratings are cumulative. A handful of poor tests in a row visibly reduces invitation volume, which is why the pattern matters more than any single rejection.

The five causes

CauseSymptomFix
Silent or clipped audioMic muted, or voice drops outTest the mic before every session
Off-briefYou did a different task than askedRead the brief twice, follow order
Too shortFinished in a third of expected timeSlow down; think aloud
No friction reportedEverything was 'easy'Describe confusion honestly
Technical failureBrowser crashed, tab closedCheck hardware requirements first

Audio is almost always the culprit

In practice the majority of rejections are audio problems: a muted mic, a headset that was not selected in the recorder, or background noise that drowns the narration.

Record ten seconds of yourself before starting the real test and play it back. This one habit prevents more rejections than any technique.

What a good recording sounds like

Continuous narration, in the first person, describing expectation and reaction: "I expect the save button to be top right... it is not there, I am looking in the menu... ah, it is under a gear icon, which I did not expect."

What fails is either silence with clicking, or a performance: smooth completion plus "that was intuitive", which tells the client nothing and reads as low effort.

Appeals and ratings

If a rejection is clearly wrong, such as a technical failure on their side, contact support once with the test ID and a short factual explanation. Appeals succeed when they are specific and fail when they are indignant.

If the rejection is fair, do not appeal. Move to the next test, because the recovery mechanism here is consistent quality rather than argument.

Protecting the rating

  • Run the equipment check before your first test of the day.
  • Keep the room quiet; a fan is enough to fail a review.
  • Never accept a test you cannot finish in the stated time.
  • Read the brief to the end before clicking record.
  • Submit one test at a time rather than stacking sessions.

Frequently asked

Does one rejected test get me banned?

No. Repeated low-quality tests reduce invitations, and a pattern of them can end access, but a single fair rejection is not a ban.

Can I redo a rejected test?

Usually not the same one; the slot is reassigned. You can appeal if the failure was technical on the platform's side.

How many tests before my rating drops?

Ratings are cumulative and not published in detail. A few poor tests in a row is enough to be noticeable in invitation volume.
