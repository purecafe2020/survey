Do Survey Sites Sell Your Data? What Actually Happens

The question people mean is "will my phone number end up on a spam list". The honest answer: legitimate research platforms trade in aggregate statistics, while scam lookalikes harvest identities. The difference is visible in the privacy policy.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

The business model decides the answer

A research panel is paid by brands for insight, not for contact lists. Selling individual identities would destroy the panel's own asset, because respondents would leave and clients would receive unrepresentative data.

So what they sell is the aggregate: "62% of urban parents aged 30-39 prefer X". Your individual answers are used to build that number, not passed on as a record of you.

What actually leaves the platform

DataTypically shared with clients
Aggregated survey resultsYes, in full
Anonymised individual responsesOften, with identifiers stripped
Your name, email, phoneNo, under most panel terms
Your addressOnly for product home tests, and only for shipping
Payment detailsNo, held by the payment processor

Where it gets murkier

Three grey areas are worth knowing about. Profiling: platforms build a picture of you to match studies, which is internal but detailed. Advertising: some panels run ad networks and use cookie data for targeting, which is disclosed in the policy and usually opt-out-able. Partners: "trusted partners" clauses can be broad, and they are the part of a policy actually worth reading.

None of this equals selling your identity, but it does mean a detailed commercial profile exists with your name attached.

How to read a privacy policy in three minutes

  1. Search for the words "sell", "share" and "third parties". Note whether they are tied to personal data or aggregate data.
  2. Look for a named operating company and a physical address. Scam sites never have both.
  3. Check whether there is a real opt-out or deletion route, and whether GDPR or CCPA rights are mentioned.
  4. Find the data retention period. "Indefinitely" with no justification is a warning sign.

Your actual rights

If you are in the EU, UK or California, you can request access to your data, correction and deletion, and you can object to certain processing. Platforms serving those markets must provide a route, usually an email address in the policy.

Elsewhere, the practical protection is choosing platforms with published terms and named companies, and never giving an ID number, bank password or verification code to a survey.

The real risk is not the panel

Identity theft in this space comes from lookalike sites that promise $500 and ask for a card number, and from text-message campaigns impersonating research recruiters. Both are structurally different from a panel: they ask for money or credentials before anything else.

Judge by sequence. Money flows toward you on a real panel; it flows away from you on a scam.

Frequently asked

Do survey sites sell my name and phone number?

Reputable panels generally do not; they sell aggregate results. Scam lookalikes harvest exactly that, which is why the operating company and privacy policy matter.

Can I opt out of data sharing?

Usually yes for marketing uses, and GDPR/CCPA regions have statutory rights. Look for an opt-out or deletion route in the policy.

Is it safe to give my real address?

Only when a product home test needs to ship something to you. Otherwise, a city and postcode district is sufficient for matching.
