Diary Studies: What They Pay and How Not to Fail One

A diary study pays you to live normally for a week and describe it. The daily effort is small, the total fee is decent, and the failure mode is almost always forgetting rather than inability.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

What a diary study actually is

Researchers want to know how something is used in real life over time, not what you think of it in a one-hour interview. So they ask you to log scenarios, feelings and friction over three to fourteen days, typically in a mobile app or a shared document.

Entries take three to fifteen minutes: a short text answer, sometimes a photo, occasionally a thirty-second video. The total commitment is under two hours spread across the period.

What it pays

LengthTypical total fee
3-5 days$50-120
7 days$89-200
10-14 days$150-300+

Compared with a one-hour session at $75, a seven-day diary at $150 works out better per hour of attention, which is why it suits people with a stable routine.

Why people fail on day five

Almost every abandoned diary study is abandoned for the same reason: the novelty wears off and the reminder gets swiped away. Missing a single entry can void the whole study on some projects, which converts a $150 opportunity into nothing.

The fix is mechanical, not motivational: put a recurring alarm at the same time each day, and log as the thing happens rather than saving it for the evening.

Making entries that survive review

Thin entries get marked as incomplete. "Used it normally" is thin. "Used it at 7.40am while making breakfast; the lid jammed and I used a spoon instead" is not, and takes the same twenty seconds to write.

Photograph the actual object in the actual place: the bottle on the counter, the app screen mid-task, the packaging in the bin. Generic stock-style photos read as generated and are rejected.

Who this suits

People with predictable routines: remote workers, parents at home, students, anyone who is in the same environment most days. It also suits people who express themselves better in writing than live, since there is no moderator watching you think.

It does not suit frequent travellers or anyone whose week could change without notice. Declining is better than abandoning, because field houses and platforms both record reliability.

Home tests are the close cousin

Product tests ship an item to your home for one to four weeks, with questionnaires, photos and sometimes a closing interview. Fees run $50 to $200 equivalent plus the product itself.

The same discipline applies and the same trap waits: use the product properly, because the closing questions are specific. "It was nice" collapses the moment they ask how many pumps you used per wash.

Frequently asked

How much do diary studies pay?

Roughly $50-120 for three to five days, $89-200 for a week, and $150-300+ for two weeks. Fees vary by market and segment scarcity.

Do I need to record video?

Sometimes. Many studies accept text and photos; some ask for short video entries. The format is stated before you accept, so check it fits your privacy comfort.

What happens if I miss a day?

It depends on the project. Some allow catch-up entries, others void the study. Assume it voids until the instructions say otherwise, and set an alarm from day one.
