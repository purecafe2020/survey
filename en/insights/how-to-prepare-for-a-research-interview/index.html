How to Prepare for a Paid Research Interview

Most people over-prepare the wrong things. They rehearse opinions and forget the two things the researcher actually needs: concrete stories, and a working microphone.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

Before the day

  • Read the invitation twice. It states the format, the length, and often what not to prepare. One line usually says whether notes are allowed.
  • Confirm the time zone. The most common avoidable no-show in this industry is a time zone mistake, not a forgotten session.
  • Check the money side. Confirm the incentive and how it is paid, in writing, before you block the hour.

Ten minutes before

Three mechanical checks decide whether the session survives its first minute: the meeting link opens, the microphone works, and the room is quiet enough. Test the mic by recording ten seconds on your phone and playing it back.

Join two minutes early. Not ten, because you will sit in a waiting room wondering whether you are in the wrong place, and not on the dot, because the moderator is usually juggling other participants.

The opening sixty seconds

Almost every interview starts the same way: "tell me a bit about yourself and your role." Have that answer ready in three sentences covering who you are, what you do, and one detail relevant to the topic.

Do not recite a CV. The moderator is checking that you are real and that your profile matches, and then moving on. Ramble here and you spend your own paid time on nothing.

How to answer well

The pattern that works: a concrete instance, then the judgement, then the cost. "Last March we tried X. It took three weeks longer than planned because Y. In the end we went back to Z."

Opinions are cheap and interchangeable. Specifics — dates, tools, numbers, the name of the thing that annoyed you — are what the client is paying for, and what a model cannot supply.

Saying "I don't know" is fine and often better than inventing. Researchers prefer an honest gap to a plausible guess, because the guess contaminates their data.

After the session

Confirm the incentive and when it lands: platform credit within days, or a transfer after attendance is confirmed. If it is coming by transfer, ask for the confirmation in a message so you have a record.

Note the study name and date. When you have done twenty of these, you will not remember which client still owes you $75.

Frequently asked

How long do research interviews last?

Usually 30 to 90 minutes. The invitation states it, and the fee is set against that estimate, so treat the stated length as the commitment.

Can I say I don't know?

Yes, and you should. An honest gap is more useful to a researcher than an invented answer, which corrupts the data they are paying for.

Should I prepare notes?

Only if the invitation allows it. Even then, use bullet reminders rather than sentences; a script kills the follow-up questions that justify the fee.
