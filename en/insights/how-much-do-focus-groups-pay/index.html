How Much Do Paid Focus Groups Pay in 2026?

Focus groups are the most advertised and most misrepresented format in paid research. Adverts promise $500; the honest range for a ninety-minute session is $75 to $350, and where you land depends on four variables.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

Real rates by format

FormatLengthTypical fee (US)
Online focus group60-90 min$75-150
In-person focus group90-120 min$100-250
Focus group with specialist audience90 min$200-350+
Online bulletin board (multi-day)3-5 days$100-250
Paired or triad interview60-90 min$100-200

Figures reflect US recruitment through established platforms. Markets with fewer participants pay less in nominal terms but are easier to get into.

The four variables behind the fee

  • Segment scarcity. "Parents of children under two who use a specific formula" pays far more than "adults who drink coffee".
  • Length. Ninety minutes is the standard unit; two hours commands a premium.
  • Location. In-person sessions in expensive cities include travel compensation.
  • Who is paying. A client recruiting directly through a research platform usually pays more than a panel aggregating traffic.

Online versus in person

Online groups now dominate because they remove geography and cost the recruiter less. The trade is roughly 20-30% lower fees than in person, in exchange for no travel and much wider eligibility.

If you live outside a major city, online groups are effectively your only route, and they are a good one: the same ninety minutes from your kitchen table, no commute, no parking.

How and when you get paid

Three patterns exist: instant digital incentive after the session (gift card or transfer, common on platforms), payment within one to two weeks (typical for agency recruitment), and cash on the day (common for in-person fieldwork outside the US).

Whatever the method, the amount should be stated before you agree to attend. A session that reveals the fee only after you arrive is not a research session you want.

What a month realistically looks like

For a general consumer profile in a major market, one to three sessions a month is normal, so roughly $100 to $400. Specialist profiles get fewer invitations at higher fees. Nobody gets four sessions a week; supply is set by how many studies happen to need your segment.

Red flags in focus group adverts

This format attracts the most fraud, because the promise is large and the verification is invisible. Walk away when:

  • You are asked to pay a registration or "seat reservation" fee.
  • The advert promises a fixed $500 for an unspecified session.
  • The recruiter asks for a card number "to hold your place".
  • You cannot find a company name, privacy notice or physical address anywhere.
  • The invitation arrives by unsolicited text message with a shortened link.

Legitimate recruitment is free, states the incentive upfront and names the firm doing it.

Frequently asked

How much is a two-hour focus group?

In the US, typically $150-300 for a general consumer segment and more for specialist audiences. Under two hours, $75-200.

Do online focus groups pay less?

Usually 20-30% less than in-person sessions, because the recruiter saves venue and travel costs. They are also far easier to qualify for.

Why do some adverts promise $300-500?

Some are specialist studies that genuinely pay that. Most are lead-generation pages collecting personal data, or scams. Check whether a named company is recruiting.
