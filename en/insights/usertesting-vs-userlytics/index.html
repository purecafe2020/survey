UserTesting vs Userlytics: Which Pays Better?

Both pay you to talk through a website while clicking. The difference is volume, quality enforcement and which countries they bother to serve.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

Where UserTesting wins

Volume and predictability. If you are in one of its core markets and have compliant hardware, tests appear regularly and the payment history is consistent. It is the better platform to build a habit on.

It is also stricter: null audio, abandoned tasks or a suspiciously short runtime get rejected, and rejections feed a rating that decides future invitations.

Where Userlytics wins

Coverage and occasional upside. Its wider country list means some testers who see nothing on UserTesting get work here, and the per-test range reaches higher for longer studies with interviews attached.

The trade is roughness: invitations arrive late, briefs are sometimes vague, support is slow, and volume is not dependable enough to plan around.

What this means practically

Join UserTesting first and treat it as the base. Add Userlytics as a second queue for gaps, and compare the fee before committing to any test that appears on both — the same client-side study can pay double on one platform.

Neither is a substitute for interviews. Twenty minutes at $20 is $60 an hour on paper, but the real constraint is supply, not rate.

Frequently asked

Which pays more per test?

UserTesting on average, because its volume concentrates on standardised tests. Userlytics has a higher ceiling on occasional complex studies.

Which is easier to start?

Both onboard immediately. UserTesting needs a hardware check; Userlytics is more forgiving but slower to send work.

Can I use both?

Yes, and you should. They are separate companies with separate queues. One account each.
