Mechanical Turk Has Closed: Where the Work Went

Mechanical Turk closed permanently on 30 September 2026. For two decades it was the default entry point into paid online work, paying cents per task. Its closure says something about where this whole category is going.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

What happened

Amazon announced the closure on 25 August 2026 and shut the platform on 30 September. The notice was characteristically brief: after assessing its programmes, it decided to close AWS Mechanical Turk. Workers had until 30 October 2026 to confirm payment preferences so remaining balances could be paid out.

At its peak MTurk counted more than half a million registered workers. It launched in 2005 and effectively invented the vocabulary of the gig economy: Human Intelligence Tasks, requesters, Turkers, and the idea that a few cents per micro-task could add up to a wage.

Why it closed

Two forces, both visible in the closure coverage.

Specialists took the volume. Data-labeling and AI-training firms such as Scale AI, Mercor and Prolific now win the contracts that used to be posted as anonymous batches. They offer verified participants, auditable quality and enterprise billing, none of which MTurk was designed for.

AI took the task itself. Reporting at the time noted that a large share of MTurk workers, variously put at 30-40%, were already using AI to complete the work they were paid for. Once the requesters know that, the case for paying a human by the task collapses.

Where the requesters went

Three destinations, each with a different economics.

The pattern is consistent: work that survives is either verified research or expertise-gated training. The anonymous five-cent batch is the part that died.

What it means for participants

Competition in the bottom tier got worse. Hundreds of thousands of former Turkers were looking for somewhere to go, and most landed on the same handful of panels that already had thin inventory.

The middle and top tiers are unaffected. Interviews, focus groups, B2B studies and credentialed AI work were never on MTurk and do not care that it closed. If your income came from $2 surveys, this is a push toward the tier that actually pays.

The lesson is about platform risk. A balance held on a platform is exposure, not savings. MTurk gave one month's notice. Withdraw at the threshold, always.

How to spot the next closure early

  • Single-source demand: a platform that depends on one client or one workflow is fragile.
  • Payment friction: rising thresholds, slower payouts and new verification walls are usually cash-flow signals.
  • Task collapse: long droughts in a category that used to be steady.
  • No product investment: no new features, no communication, a dead help centre.

None of these is proof. All of them together are a reason to withdraw and diversify.

Frequently asked

Did I lose money when MTurk closed?

Balances were paid on the schedule you had configured, and workers had until 30 October 2026 to confirm payment preferences. Anyone who had not set a schedule had to contact support after that date.

What replaced MTurk?

Nothing directly. Research platforms such as Prolific absorbed the survey and AI-evaluation work, while specialist firms took the labeling contracts by offering verified workers and audit trails.

Is my tier affected?

If you do interviews, focus groups or usability tests, no. If you did micro-tasks for cents, your competition just increased substantially.
