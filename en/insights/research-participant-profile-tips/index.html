How to Write a Research Profile That Gets You Selected

Studies are matched against fields you filled in months ago and forgot about. Most people who complain about never being selected have a profile that says "office worker" and nothing else.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

The profile is the matching engine

Recruiters do not read your profile as prose; they filter it as data. "Occupation: office worker" cannot match a study recruiting supply chain managers, because the filter looks for the string and the category, not the vibe.

This is the cheapest improvement available to any participant: twenty minutes of accurate field-filling changes what you are offered for years.

Specific does not mean inflated

There is a difference between precision and exaggeration. "I raise requirements but a VP approves the budget" is precise and qualifies you for influencer-tier studies, which pay well. "I am the decision-maker" when you are not gets exposed in the first five minutes of the session.

The rule: describe your real situation in the most concrete terms available. Precision is what recruiters are filtering for, not seniority.

Consistency across platforms

The same facts should appear on every platform, and on LinkedIn if you use it. Contradictions, such as a job title that changes between platforms, read as fabrication to a reviewer even when they are just stale data.

When you change roles, update all of them in the same week. Stale titles are the second most common cause of B2B rejection after the email itself.

How often to revisit it

Quarterly is enough, plus immediately after any life change: new job, new child, move, new car. Those are exactly the events that open new study categories, and they are also when a profile most easily goes stale.

Set a calendar reminder. It is fifteen minutes, four times a year, and it is the highest-return task in this whole activity.

What never to fake

Do not invent a role, a diagnosis, a purchase or a household member. Every one of these gets probed in a live session, and a mismatch ends the session without payment plus an account flag.

If you do not fit a category, the correct move is to skip those studies and focus where you do fit, which is where your hit rate and your rate per hour both improve.

Frequently asked

Should I exaggerate my job title to get B2B studies?

No. Verification checks work email and LinkedIn, and the session probes specifics. An accurate influencer-level description qualifies you honestly for well-paid studies.

How often should I update my profile?

Quarterly, plus immediately after a job change, a move or a new child. Those events create and remove study eligibility.

Does adding a photo help?

Rarely for matching, which is field-based. It can matter for in-person sessions where the recruiter needs to identify you at check-in.
