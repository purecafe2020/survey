Synthetic Respondents: The Real Threat to Survey Income

The thing most likely to reduce your survey income is not a worse platform. It is a client deciding that a synthetic respondent is good enough for the question they were going to pay you for.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

What synthetic respondents are

A synthetic respondent is a model built from real interviews and category data, then queried as if it were a person. In August 2026 a company launched a marketplace of more than a thousand such "consumer twins" for a single category, priced at roughly $10 per twin per study, against a reported $20,000+ for a traditional study.

Separately, a service launched in October 2026 in which a person's own AI assistant answers paid consumer questions, with the human approving what is shared. That is a different model, but the direction is the same: the answer gets produced without a person sitting through a questionnaire.

Which budgets actually move

Research typeRisk from syntheticWhy
Concept and packaging screeningHighBroad, directional, cheap to get wrong
Message and ad pre-testingHighDirectional rather than evidential
Large-scale tracking surveysMediumStill needs real panel data for benchmarks
In-depth interviewsLowClients want a person to push back
B2B and regulated categoriesLowAccountability and verification requirements
Usability testingLowRequires real behaviour on a real interface

Note the shape: what moves is cheap, broad and directional. What stays human is expensive, narrow and accountable.

Why clients are tempted

Not primarily accuracy. Speed and price. A synthetic study answers in hours for the cost of a coffee, and for a decision that would have been made on gut feel anyway, that is a better trade than a two-week panel study.

There is also a political motive: nobody gets blamed for a synthetic sample the way they get blamed for a real one that missed the market.

Where it breaks down

Synthetic respondents are built from what people said before, so they are structurally unable to report something genuinely new: a product nobody has used, a price point nobody has seen, a workaround that has not been invented yet. That is precisely the research worth paying for.

There are also unresolved questions about consent, since twins are built from real interviews, and about whether an approving human counts as a respondent at all. Expect this to be litigated and regulated before it settles.

What to do about it

  1. Move up the value chain: interviews, usability, diaries and B2B work are the least exposed, and they already pay the most.
  2. Be the person who can be asked follow-ups: the one thing a model cannot do is improvise convincingly about a specific Tuesday at work.
  3. Do not build a business on $2 surveys: that is the tier with the highest substitution risk and the lowest margin.

Frequently asked

Will synthetic respondents replace panels?

Not the whole category. They are displacing cheap directional testing first. Interviews, usability and B2B work remain human because clients need accountability and genuine novelty.

Should I avoid consumer surveys now?

No, but do not build your income on them. Treat consumer panels as filler and put your effort into units of work that require a real person.

Is the data any good?

It is plausible rather than true. Synthetic samples reproduce what has already been said, which is fine for direction and unreliable for discovery.
