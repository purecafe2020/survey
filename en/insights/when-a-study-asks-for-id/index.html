When a Study Asks for Your ID or a Selfie

Being asked for a passport photo to answer a survey feels wrong. On platforms paying $200 a session it is also normal — which is exactly why the details of what you send matter.

Verified：November 2026 About 2 min read

Why verification exists

Three reasons, all commercial. Researchers are paying for a specific person, so a verified identity protects the data they are buying. Platforms lose money to duplicate accounts. And the highest fees attract fraud, which means the platforms with the best rates check hardest.

So a request for ID is not automatically a red flag. What matters is who is asking, over what channel, and what they keep.

What normal looks like

RequestAssessment
Government ID uploaded inside the platformNormal on research platforms
A selfie or short video for livenessNormal, increasingly common
Proof of address for payoutNormal once earnings exceed a threshold
Work email for B2B verificationNormal, and expected

What is never normal

  • ID by email or chat attachment. Legitimate platforms use an upload form with a stated retention policy.
  • A bank password, card number or SMS code. Never. No exception, in any category.
  • A "refundable deposit" to verify you. That is a fee, and it is a scam.
  • ID sent to a person who messaged you first, rather than through the platform you registered with.

How to limit what you give

You cannot opt out of verification on the platforms that require it, but you can reduce exposure:

  1. Use the platform's own upload, never a messaging app.
  2. Ask what is retained and for how long. A platform with a real privacy policy can answer; a scam cannot.
  3. Where a platform accepts a redacted document, redact fields it does not need, keeping name, photo and expiry visible.
  4. Keep a note of where and when you verified, so a later "verification" request can be checked against it.

If you are uncomfortable

Then do not use that platform. Verification is a condition of access, not a negotiation, and the honest trade is: the top-paying work requires proof of identity. Usability tests and consumer panels mostly do not, and they are where to go if you would rather not upload documents.

What you should never do is send a document to someone outside the platform to "speed things up". That is how a verified identity becomes a stolen one.

Frequently asked

Is it safe to upload my ID to a research platform?

It is the norm on the higher-paying platforms, and safer than sending it to an individual. Check for an upload form and a stated retention policy.

Can I refuse and still participate?

On platforms that require verification, no. Use usability testing and consumer panels instead if you would rather not hand over documents.

What if I do not have a passport?

Most accept a national ID or driving licence. If a specific document is mandatory and you lack it, ask support before assuming you are excluded.
