Paid Survey FAQ: 12 Answers Beginners Actually Need

These are the questions people actually search: is it legal, how much, why do I get kicked out, is my data safe. Straight answers, no circling.

Verified：November 2026

Legitimacy and reality

1. Is it legal?

Yes. Market research is a mature industry and brands genuinely pay for consumer opinion. What is illegal is the fraud that borrows the industry's name to collect fees or harvest data.

2. Can you actually earn money?

Yes, but little. Mainstream panels work out to $1 to $4 an hour, research platforms with enforced floors reach $8 or more, and Chinese panels pay 5 to 15 CNY. Accurate as pocket money, wrong as income.

3. Why do people post huge earnings screenshots?

Three possibilities: exaggeration, stacking several platforms and several months into one "month", or straight lead-generation advertising. Real high earnings in this space come from crowdsourcing or freelancing, not questionnaires.

Screen-outs and efficiency

4. Why do I keep getting screened out?

Quotas. A survey targeting "urban parents aged 30 to 40 with two children" may have only 100 slots, which close when full. Being screened out usually says nothing about your answers; the seat was simply taken. To reduce it: complete your profile, work during release windows, and prioritise surveys you clearly match.

5. Is there compensation for a screen-out?

Mostly no. A few platforms pay a few cents if you are screened out mid-way. It is industry practice and complaints do not change it.

6. How many can I do per day?

It depends on platform and profile. Mainstream panels yield three to ten on a good day; research platforms may offer a handful per week. Doing too many triggers quality review, so volume-farming backfires.

7. Can I use a bot or auto-answer script?

No. Platforms run behavioural detection; the outcome is invalid samples, a ban and a forfeited balance. Such tools also frequently carry malware.

Privacy and safety

8. Is it safe to give personal information?

Legitimate panels ask for basic demographics such as age, region, household structure and income band, because matching requires it. They do not need your ID number, bank card, SMS code or social account password. Anyone requesting those four is running a scam.

9. Will my answers be published?

Not individually. Results appear as aggregate statistics, though platforms retain individual responses for analysis. The exact scope is in each privacy policy, and two minutes spent reading the data-use clause before registering is time well spent.

10. Will my data be sold?

Legitimate platforms supply anonymised aggregate data to clients, not your identity. But "legitimate" needs verification: a clear privacy policy, registration in a regulated jurisdiction, and a contactable company entity.

Regions and tax

11. Can users in mainland China join international panels?

Mostly not, compliantly. Prolific is limited to most OECD countries, Survey Junkie and Branded Surveys concentrate on North America, and Prime Opinion's 71 markets do not include mainland China. Proxy spoofing or buying an overseas identity breaches platform terms and results in a ban with the balance forfeited. The realistic options for users in mainland China are domestic survey and crowdsourcing platforms, which is the assumption behind this site.

12. Do I owe tax?

It depends on amount and jurisdiction. US platforms generally treat participants as independent contractors and issue 1099 forms above a threshold; Chinese crowdsourcing platforms usually withhold labour-income tax. Small amounts rarely trigger anything, but if you accumulate more, keep records and follow local filing rules.

Is paid surveying legal?

Yes. Market research is an established industry with real budgets. Fraud that borrows its name to collect fees or harvest data is the illegal part.

How much can I earn per month?

Mainstream panels pay an effective $1 to $4 per hour, research platforms with enforced floors pay $8 or more, and Chinese panels pay about 5 to 15 CNY. Reasonable pocket money, unrealistic income.

Why do I keep getting screened out?

Surveys have quotas; when the target segment fills, the study closes. It usually has nothing to do with answer quality.

Do I need to pay to start?

Never. Legitimate panels are free to join; anything asking for a membership fee, deposit or unlock charge is a scam.

Will I be asked for ID or bank details?

Not at registration. Some platforms verify identity at first withdrawal, but none should ever ask for SMS codes or passwords.

Can users in mainland China participate?

Most international panels do not support it, and proxies or false identities breach their terms. Domestic survey and crowdsourcing platforms are the usable route.

How long do payouts take?

PayPal usually 1 to 3 business days, bank transfer 3 to 10, gift cards instant to a few days, and domestic wallets instant to the following week.

Can I automate surveys?

No. Behavioural detection flags invalid samples and bans accounts without refunding balances, and such tools often carry malware.

Can I run multiple accounts?

No. Accounts linked by device or payment method are the most common ban trigger, and balances are usually forfeited.

Is my data sold?

Legitimate platforms supply anonymised aggregate data rather than your identity. Read the data-use clause in the privacy policy before registering.

Do I owe tax on this income?

It depends on amount and jurisdiction. US platforms issue 1099 forms above a threshold, and Chinese platforms usually withhold labour-income tax.

Which platform should I start with?

It depends on where you live. Where eligible, Prolific pays best; elsewhere start with mainstream panels that have low thresholds and steady volume.
