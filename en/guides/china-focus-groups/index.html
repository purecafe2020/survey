China Focus Groups: Cities, Recruiters, Process and Scam Checks

The Chinese route works differently: there is no public listing like Respondent. Recruitment runs through local field supervisors, recruiters and WeChat accounts, so knowing the process matters more than knowing a platform.

Verified：November 2026

Who recruits: three kinds of organisation

Branches of global research firms: Ipsos, Kantar, NielsenIQ and similar. Tight process and transparent fees, though recruitment is usually handled by local fieldwork partners.

Domestic research and consultancy firms: iResearch, Horizon, CTR Market Research and many smaller shops. Brands commission them, and they define the sample structure.

Local field houses and supervisor teams: the people who actually call you and book you in. They maintain respondent databases, keeping people on file for recurring criteria.

Your goal is to get into those databases, not to hunt for projects from scratch each time.

Which cities have work

Density runs roughly: Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Shenzhen, which account for the majority; then Chengdu, Hangzhou, Wuhan, Nanjing, Xi'an, Chongqing, Tianjin, Qingdao and Changsha.

Tier-three cities and below see fewer projects, but one category specifically recruits lower-tier market respondents, where competition is lighter and fees are not low. If you live outside the big cities, that is your angle.

Online focus groups, now routine, removed the geography problem: ninety minutes on Zoom or Tencent Meeting typically pays 150-400 CNY and is open nationwide. This segment has grown quickly.

Where to sign up

  • WeChat official accounts: search for focus group recruitment plus a city name; many field houses post continuously.
  • Recruiter WeChat contacts: once in a database, they message you directly, which is far faster than public posts and the most effective route here.
  • Local field house websites and phones: established firms accept registrations year-round via a long intake form.
  • Mall intercepts and community recruitment: common for offline studies, especially baby and FMCG categories.

Note: legitimate recruitment never charges a fee and never asks you to recruit others. Anyone asking for money or downlines should be blocked.

What the process looks like

  1. Intake form: twenty to forty questions on age, occupation, income, household, spending habits and brands. Detail drives matching.
  2. Screening call: a supervisor verifies by phone, asks more granular questions and confirms you can attend.
  3. Invitation: time and place sent by WeChat or SMS, usually two to five days ahead.
  4. Check-in: ID verification and a confidentiality agreement.
  5. Discussion, 90-120 minutes: typically six to eight people, with a moderator, one-way mirror or recording.
  6. Payment on site: cash, WeChat or transfer, usually the same day.

Treat any "500 CNY, anyone can join" post with suspicion: it is either a specialist study or a scam.

Five traps to avoid

  • Registration fees or deposits: legitimate recruitment is free; a fee means fraud.
  • Referral requirements: paying you only if you recruit others is a pyramid structure.
  • ID scans plus bank cards: identity checks do not need these, so treat the request as high risk.
  • Fee cuts on arrival: legitimate firms confirm incentives in writing; walk out if the number changes.
  • Anonymous groups: if you cannot find the field house's registered name, do not submit detailed personal data.

Also be careful about applying to the same study through several field houses; being caught gets you blacklisted, which costs far more than one session.
