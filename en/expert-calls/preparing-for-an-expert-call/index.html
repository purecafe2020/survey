Preparing for an Expert Call: What to Say in 60 Minutes

An expert call is neither an exam nor a speech. The buyer is purchasing an hour of your judgement, so structure beats breadth.

Verified：November 2026

Thirty minutes of preparation

  1. Read the topic and the buyer context. Invitations usually state the focus: market size, competitive landscape, procurement flow, channel structure.
  2. Draft three to five structures. For example: how many steps procurement runs, who signs at each, and the average cycle length. Structure survives interruption.
  3. Have two numbers ready. The most common questions are order-of-magnitude: unit price bands, annual spend, gross margin range. Give ranges, not precise figures.

The first minute: position yourself correctly

The buyer first confirms your vantage point: which company, which role, what you own, and for how long. Answer and stop; do not narrate company history.

A useful formula: "I have been here X years, I own Y, and I can speak to Z; I am not close to A, and would defer on that." Declaring the boundary increases credibility rather than reducing it.

Allocating the hour

WindowContent
0-5 minIdentity and vantage point
5-35 minTheir main line: market, competition, process
35-50 minDetail probing: numbers, examples, counter-examples
50-60 minTheir summary; your closing "what should I have asked?"

That closing question frequently produces a second invitation.

Three hard boundaries

  • Unpublished operating data. Revenue, margin, contract terms, unreleased products: off limits, even if they say "just a rough idea".
  • Identifiable personal data. Customer lists, patient information, individual pay details.
  • Prices and quotation documents. Specific supplier pricing is a textbook compliance trap.

The standard deflection: "I can't discuss that figure, but I can explain how the process works." Moving from numbers to mechanism satisfies both sides.

Frequently asked

Is the first call hard?

Not hard, but it will probe detail. Three to five prepared structures beat memorising numbers.

What if they ask something I cannot discuss?

Say it is not something you can discuss, then redirect to process or mechanism. Buyers generally accept the substitution.

Can I say I don't know?

Yes, and it is far safer than inventing. Declaring a gap makes your other answers more credible.
