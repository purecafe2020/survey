Expert calls: $150-1,000 an hour for industry judgement

Before committing capital, funds and consultancies pay someone who has actually done the job to talk for 45-60 minutes. The gate is not a degree; it is whether you have a verifiable stretch of relevant experience.

What is actually being bought

A PE fund buying an industrial valve maker needs to know the real gross margin in that niche, how the top customers actually run procurement, and where a new entrant gets stuck. None of that is in public reports; only someone who worked inside can answer it.

So it pays for an hour. The buyers are funds, consultancies and corporate strategy teams; the sellers are mid-level and senior practitioners. The price reaches hundreds or thousands an hour because the judgement maps onto a decision worth millions, not because your time is intrinsically valuable.

Which is the point: you are not selling hours, you are selling "I have hit that wall". Selection therefore depends on whether your experience can be verified and sliced into specific questions.

What you get, and what is taken in between

By seniority, third-party compilations put it at roughly $150-300/hr for mid-level professionals (5-10 years), $300-500 for directors (10+ years), and $500-1,000+ for VPs, C-suite or genuinely scarce specialists. Calls usually run 30-60 minutes and are prorated.

Clients pay more. For GLG, third-party estimates put the client side at $1,000-2,500/hr; the spread is the network's revenue. Some platforms take zero from the expert and charge only the client, but most do not publish it.

Expect delayed payment: two to six weeks after the call is normal, by bank transfer. That is the settlement cycle, not a withholding.

The gate is verifiability, not credentials

No one checks your university. Three things must line up:

  • Record: employer, title and dates findable on LinkedIn or a company site.
  • Depth: decisions you personally handled, such as "I approved purchases under 5 million" or "I ran the regional channel rebuild".
  • Answerability: your experience can be cut into 45-minute questions, rather than "I have been in this industry for years".

The commonest rejection is a title that is too generic. "Marketing specialist" or "operations manager" gives the system nothing to map; "regional sales director, FMCG, team of 12, 80M quota" changes the match rate immediately.

Why this is reachable from China

This is less out of reach than it looks:

  • There are networks built on mainland China sourcing. Capvision specialises in exactly that, with coverage Western networks cannot match.
  • Language is not the barrier. Many calls run in Chinese, with a bilingual analyst on the client side.
  • Cross-border payment is routine. Mostly bank transfer; you declare the income where you are tax resident.

The real barrier is elsewhere: you have to have held a role where you were accountable for outcomes. That is not something study can fix.

Where people actually fall out

Approval is the start; three things end careers here:

  1. The compliance attestation. Nearly every network makes you confirm you are not breaching confidentiality. Ticking it casually and then talking too much gets you permanently flagged, which is the most serious blacklist in this business.
  2. Your rate. Too low (say $50/hr) reads as implausible seniority; too high never matches. For a first call, sit at the median of your band.
  3. Time zones and cancellations. Buyers call on their own working hours. Accepting and then cancelling affects future invitations.

One line: this filter selects reliable professionals, not eager earners.
