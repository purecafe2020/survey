From Signup to Your First Paid Call: The Expert-Network Path

The counter-intuitive part: you do not hunt for work, you make yourself findable and verifiable.

Verified：November 2026

Step one: three self-checks before you register

Before registering, test yourself with three sentences:

  1. How many consecutive years in this industry? Under five rarely matches; ten-plus brings steady invitations.
  2. Can you name three details only an insider knows? Real margin structure, the customer's procurement approval chain, why a certain project type always slips. If not, do not apply yet.
  3. Is your record findable online? LinkedIn, a company team page, trade press. At least one must line up, or the system has nothing to match.

All three pass, continue. If one fails, the gap is accumulation, not technique.

Step three: where to set your rate

Rate is neither "higher is better" nor "lower gets picked". Workable starting points by band:

  • 5-10 years, functional specialist: $150-250/hr
  • 10+ years, director or head of: $300-500
  • VP, C-suite or scarce niche: $500-1,000

Three rules: never go below the floor (a $50 rate reads as implausible), quote the median on your first call and raise it after two or three completed calls, and stay consistent within a network, because wildly different rates across projects get flagged.

Note that this is an hourly figure prorated to call length. A 30-minute call pays half.

Step four: how the first call runs

Calls run 30-60 minutes with an analyst or consultant holding a question list. No slides needed, but structure helps:

  1. Open in two minutes: one line on your background and what you can cover. Skip the pleasantries.
  2. Body: answer the list, and after each point add "the trap here is...". That sentence is where the value sits, because only an insider can say it.
  3. Say you do not know. A guessed number gets cross-checked, exposed and noted.
  4. Close by asking what was not covered; it opens the door to the next call.

There is usually a short post-call form, sometimes confirming nothing confidential was shared. Fill it carefully; it affects future allocation.

Three red lines that end it permanently

  • No confidential information: former employer client lists, unpublished financials, live deal terms, internal pricing. The test is what the contract says, not what feels unimportant.
  • No material non-public information: if your employer is listed, anything unpublished and price-sensitive is off limits, and in several jurisdictions that is a criminal matter.
  • No invented history: inflated titles, stretched dates, borrowed experience. Verification catches it and the flag propagates across networks.

These are not "be careful" items; one strike ends it. The pay is high precisely because the whole thing runs on trust, and trust has no salvage value.

The honest closing note: this is not for people looking for a side hustle. It is for people already inside an industry who do not realise their experience is sellable. If that is you, fixing LinkedIn is worth more than ten articles.

Frequently asked

Do I need to be at a big company?

No. Buyers want your specific vantage point, not your employer's brand. Regional distributors, plant supervisors at mid-size factories and buyers at local chains are all recruited regularly. Senior executives at large listed firms are often the ones who cannot take calls.

Are calls recorded?

Usually, and transcribed for the client's internal research. You are told beforehand and must consent. If your employment contract forbids it, decline that call rather than explaining afterwards.

How soon is the first payment?

Typically two to six weeks after the call by bank transfer, sometimes slower the first time while payment details are set up. Do not discuss specific fees publicly; many networks prohibit it in their terms.

Can I take calls if my English is weak?

Ask for a Mandarin call; most networks can arrange a bilingual analyst. On technical topics Chinese may even be more accurate. Language is not the main gate; clarity about the business is.
