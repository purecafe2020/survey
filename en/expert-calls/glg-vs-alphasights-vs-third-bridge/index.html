GLG vs AlphaSights vs Third Bridge: Which One to Join

"Which is best" is the wrong question. The useful one: do you need volume or price, and can you match its rhythm.

Verified：November 2026

They are not selling the same thing

All three are expert networks, but they operate differently:

  • GLG: largest, broadest topic coverage, heaviest process;
  • AlphaSights: service-led, fastest matching, expects you to be available;
  • Third Bridge: investment-grade primary research, experts set the rate, clearest payout terms.

So it is not "pick the best"; it is "pick the one whose rhythm you can hold".

GLG: broad, but slow

The largest of the group, with the widest industry and topic coverage, from consumer goods to industrial equipment. Client-side pricing sits in the top band (third-party estimates commonly $1,000-2,500 an hour).

The cost is process weight: signup, verification, compliance questionnaires, pre-call confirmations. It suits a strong record, broad coverage and patience with process. If you need a first call within two weeks, it may not be the fastest route.

AlphaSights: fast, but it wants you available

Positioned as service-led: a client needs someone, it finds them quickly. Third-party ranges put expert fees at $250-2,500 an hour, with most calls at $250-500.

The signature is a tight window: an invitation in the morning, a call in the afternoon. For someone in full-time work that cuts both ways, with more opportunities but a need to clear an hour at short notice. People who reschedule often get filtered out quickly here.

Third Bridge: narrower, but you set the rate

Focused on investment-grade research for hedge funds, PE and consultancies. Experts set their own rate, commonly $300-500 an hour, with mid-level at $150-300 and VP or C-level at $500-1,000+.

Payout terms are the clearest of the three: about two to six weeks after the call, prorated. Briefs are more concentrated (due diligence, competitive landscape, industry structure), so if your background maps onto an investment thesis, this is where pricing is most negotiable.

How many should you join?

Two, three at most. Three reasons:

  • Different topic pools complement each other, especially in a niche industry;
  • Compliance questionnaires and verification repeat, and too many will wear you down;
  • Joining five and failing to deliver on all of them degrades your record everywhere.

A workable pair is GLG for breadth plus AlphaSights or Third Bridge for speed or pricing. Two running well beats five half-alive.

Two more names worth knowing

  • Guidepoint: broad coverage at lower cost, mixing surveys and calls; good for building a record early.
  • Capvision: strong on mainland China and Asia sourcing, and the most realistic entry point for Chinese readers, since it has local industry detail Western networks cannot source.

If you work in a local Chinese industry such as manufacturing, retail, property or pharma distribution, Capvision will often match you faster than GLG. That is not a downgrade; it is the right door.

One-line answer

For volume, AlphaSights. For price, Third Bridge. For breadth, GLG. For local Chinese industry, Capvision first.

Then stop deliberating about adding one more. The bottleneck here is never account count; it is clearing an hour at the right moment and saying something only an insider can say.

Frequently asked

Can I take the same topic through two networks?

You can be registered with both, but if two networks bring the same topic, take one and tell the other honestly that it is already covered. Taking two calls on the same subject is one of the classic violations in this business.

Does rejection at one affect the others?

Not for verification issues. Compliance flags may, since the industry shares diligence practice. So "try another network" works for record problems, never for red-line ones.

Can I join these with weak English?

Yes, especially on China-related topics where buyers often ask for a Mandarin call. But on global industry-structure briefs, English fluency directly affects the rate you can command.
